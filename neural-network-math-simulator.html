📥 Step 1: Understanding the Inputs

🔢 What are inputs and why do we normalize them?

Inputs are the pieces of information we give to the AI. They're like clues that help it make a decision!

Normalization means converting different types of measurements (like temperature in degrees, percentages, or ratings) into numbers between 0 and 1. Why? Because neural networks work best when all numbers are on the same scale - kind of like making sure everyone speaks the same language!

For example: 80% becomes 0.8, a rating of 7 out of 10 becomes 0.7, etc.

👤 Who Decides What Inputs to Use?

Humans decide! AI engineers and data scientists choose which inputs to use. This is a really important job because picking the right inputs can make the AI work great, while picking wrong inputs can make it perform poorly.

How do they decide? They ask questions like:

  • Is this information relevant? For weather prediction, cloud coverage matters. Your favorite pizza topping doesn't!
  • Can we measure it? We can measure temperature with a thermometer, but we can't easily measure "how gloomy it feels."
  • Is it fair and ethical? Some information (like race or religion) should NEVER be used as inputs because it could lead to unfair decisions.

Examples from our simulator:

  • 🌤️ Weather Predictor: Humans chose cloud coverage, temperature, and humidity because these scientifically affect rain chances.
  • 🍎 Fruit Identifier: Humans picked redness, size, and roundness because these are visual features we can see and measure.
  • 🎮 Game Recommender: Humans selected action level, strategy level, and multiplayer preference because these describe what makes games different.

Important: The AI doesn't choose its own inputs - it only works with what humans give it. This means humans have the responsibility to choose inputs wisely and fairly!

🤖 Then How Does AI Learn Which Inputs Actually Matter?

Great question! Humans choose WHAT inputs to include, but the AI learns HOW MUCH each input matters through a process called "training".

Here's how it works:

  1. Humans provide training data: Thousands of examples with correct answers (e.g., "It was 80% cloudy, 15°C, 90% humidity → and it DID rain")
  2. AI adjusts weights: The network tries different weight values to see which ones make better predictions
  3. AI discovers patterns: Through trial and error, it learns that some inputs (like cloud coverage) get BIG weights (0.8, 0.9) because they're useful, while others (like shoe size) get TINY weights (0.01, 0.02) because they don't help

The key insight: If humans accidentally include an irrelevant input (like your shoe size in a weather predictor), the AI will usually figure it out! It will assign that input a very small weight, essentially ignoring it.

Try it yourself! In our examples below, we've included some silly, unrelated inputs. Watch how the AI gives them tiny weights compared to the important inputs!

💡 Remember:
👤 Humans decide WHICH inputs to include (what goes in)
🤖 AI learns HOW MUCH each input matters (the weights)
🤝 Together they make smart predictions!

🔢 What About Those Big Numbers? (Like "GPT-70B")

You might have heard of AI models called things like "GPT-1B" or "Claude-70B" or "LLaMA-120B". What do those numbers mean?

The "B" stands for BILLION - and it's counting the WEIGHTS (parameters), not the inputs!

Let's compare to our simple example:

  • 📊 Our Weather Predictor: 5 inputs × 2 neurons = 10 weights in first layer, plus 2 more weights = 12 total weights
  • 🤖 GPT-3: Has 175 BILLION weights spread across many, many layers!
  • 🧠 Why so many? More weights = can learn more complex patterns and relationships

Think of it like this:

  • Our tiny weather network has 12 "knobs" to adjust (weights)
  • GPT-3 has 175 billion "knobs" to adjust!
  • More knobs = can learn more nuanced patterns, but also needs WAY more training data and computing power

⚡ Key Takeaway:
The "B" in "70B" or "120B" means billions of WEIGHTS (the numbers the AI learns during training), NOT billions of inputs! Our simple examples only have 3-5 inputs, but powerful AI models might still only use hundreds or thousands of inputs - they just have billions of weights to process those inputs in very sophisticated ways.

In our simulator: You'll see every single weight! In a real AI like GPT, there are too many weights to look at individually - that's why we use these simple examples to understand how they work.

💬 But Wait - How Does Text Become Numbers?

Great question! You might be wondering: "If I type 'What is the weather like in California?' to ChatGPT, how does that become numbers the neural network can use?"

The answer is a 2-step process:

Step 1: Tokenization (Breaking Text into Pieces)

  • The AI breaks your sentence into small chunks called "tokens"
  • Tokens can be whole words, parts of words, or even punctuation
  • Example: "What is the weather like in California?"
    → Tokens: ["What", " is", " the", " weather", " like", " in", " California", "?"]
  • Each token gets assigned a number from a vocabulary list (like a dictionary)
  • Example numbers: "What"=156, " is"=318, " the"=262, " weather"=6193, etc.

Step 2: Embedding (Converting to Meaningful Numbers)

  • Each token number gets converted into a LIST of many numbers called an "embedding"
  • These numbers capture the MEANING of the word
  • Example: The word "weather" might become: [0.23, -0.67, 0.89, 0.12, -0.45, 0.78, ... ] (hundreds or thousands of numbers!)
  • Similar words get similar number patterns:
    - "weather" and "climate" would have similar embeddings
    - "weather" and "pizza" would have very different embeddings

Putting it together:

Your prompt: "What is the weather like in California?"

↓ Tokenization ↓
8 tokens (8 pieces of text)

↓ Embedding ↓
8 tokens × 768 numbers each = 6,144 input numbers!
(In GPT-3, each token becomes 12,288 numbers)

↓ Neural Network ↓
These 6,144 numbers flow through the network just like our weather example!

↓ Output ↓
Network predicts the next token (word) to generate a response

The key difference from our simple examples:

  • Our weather predictor: 3-5 simple inputs (cloud coverage, temperature, etc.)
  • Language models like ChatGPT: Hundreds or thousands of inputs (from embedding each word in your prompt)
  • But the basic idea is the same! Text → Numbers → Neural Network → Prediction

🎯 Cool Fact:
When you type a 10-word sentence to ChatGPT, it might create 10,000+ input numbers! But our brain does something similar - we convert words into patterns of brain signals (electrical and chemical). The AI is inspired by how our brains process language!

Why this matters: Understanding that ALL information (text, images, sound) must become numbers before AI can process it helps you see that neural networks are always doing the same fundamental thing - taking numbers in, doing math, and producing numbers out. Whether it's predicting rain or generating text!

🧠 How does the network think?

The neural network processes information in 3 main stages:

1️⃣ Input Layer: Receives your normalized data
2️⃣ Hidden Layer: Mixes the inputs together using multiplication and addition (this is where the "thinking" happens!)
3️⃣ Output Layer: Produces final scores, then converts them to percentages
